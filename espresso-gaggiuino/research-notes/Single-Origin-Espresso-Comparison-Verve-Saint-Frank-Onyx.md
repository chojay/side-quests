
# Single-Origin Espresso Comparison: Verve vs Saint Frank vs Onyx

A deep comparison of single-origin espresso programs across three roasters: Verve Coffee Roasters, Saint Frank Coffee, and Onyx Coffee Lab. This note corrects an earlier, unpublished draft of my own that overstated Saint Frank's single-origin advantage without acknowledging that **Verve runs a robust single-origin program of its own** - the correction itself is part of the research story.

---

**Key correction to previous research:** The earlier draft positioned Saint Frank as the single-origin espresso choice and Verve as the blend choice. This is an oversimplification. Their espresso subscription explicitly includes single-origin espressos when available. Verve's single-origin program is **extensive**, not an afterthought.

## 1. Verve Single-Origin Espresso Program

### Scale & Breadth

Verve currently offers approximately **12 single-origin coffees** on their website at any given time, sourced from Ethiopia, Colombia, Honduras, Costa Rica, Mexico, and other origins. The selection rotates every Friday. This is a **larger active single-origin catalog** than Saint Frank's ~8 offerings.

**Current offerings (as of February 2026):**
- Ethiopia Tamiru Tadesse Hybrid Washed Farmlevel Reserve (150g, $38)
- Colombia Chachagüí Natural ($28.25/12oz)
- Ethiopia Tofik Nura ($26.50/12oz)
- Honduras Roberto Miranda ($27.50/12oz)
- Colombia Dulce Misterio Farmlevel Reserve (150g, $35)
- Costa Rica Granitos de Ortiz ($25.50/12oz)
- **Ethiopia Anasora Natural Espresso** ($27.50/12oz) -- **dedicated SO espresso**
- Ethiopia Mustefa Abakeno ($23/12oz)
- Mexico La Perla ($23/12oz)
- Additional rotating offerings

### Dedicated Single-Origin Espresso

Verve does not merely offer single-origins that "can be used for espresso" -- they specifically roast and label dedicated single-origin espressos. The Ethiopia Anasora Natural Espresso is explicitly described as "designed with espresso in mind," with notes of strawberry, Meyer lemon, and pine. This is a light roast (3/10 on Verve's scale) processed naturally in the Guji zone of Ethiopia.

Their espresso subscription (Roaster's Choice Espresso, $22+/12oz) includes single-origin espressos when available alongside blends like Sermon, Streetlevel, Buena Vista, and Aster.

### Farmlevel Reserve Micro-Lots

Verve's **Farmlevel Reserve** program represents their most premium single-origin tier:
- Ethiopia Tamiru Tadesse Hybrid Washed ($38/150g) -- their first African Farmlevel Reserve, grown at 2,400-2,500m, 96-hour anaerobic fermentation
- Colombia Dulce Misterio ($35/150g)

These are true micro-lots at premium pricing, comparable to what specialty-forward roasters like George Howell or Onyx charge for competition-grade coffees.

### Community Reception (Single-Origins)

Home-Barista forum members have praised Verve's single-origin espresso offerings:
- One user recalled a single-origin Duromina espresso as being exceptional
- Users with espresso subscriptions report that single-origin selections are often their favorites
- A detailed blog review of Verve's Costa Rica Sumava SOE praised it as delicate with toffee and cantaloupe notes, pulled at 203F / 18g in / 40g out on a La Spaziale S1

**The concern at scale:** At 1.5M lbs/year, Home-Barista users note Verve is "limited by lot size at that scale" and may not offer micro-lots in their regular lineup. However, the Farmlevel Reserve program directly addresses this -- those are micro-lots, just at a premium price tier separate from the main catalog.

### Roast Approach for Single-Origins

Verve roasts their single-origins **light** (3/10 on their internal scale). Their roast philosophy prioritizes origin character over roast flavor. They use Cropster tracking software on vintage Probat roasters with modern monitoring. Multi-location roasting (Santa Cruz HQ, Japan, Oxnard CA) adds complexity but they maintain consistency standards.

### Sourcing Transparency

Verve's **Farmlevel Initiative** is well-regarded:
- Often pays 50-100% above Fair Trade minimums
- Travel-based green buying with producer visits
- Global Farmlevel Summit connecting producers with each other
- Nursery Project in Urrao, Colombia (23 participating farmers, 4+ years, Caturra Chiroso variety)
- Producer names listed on single-origin bags (e.g., Tamiru Tadesse, Roberto Miranda)
- Region, elevation, and process details on product pages

**What they publish per coffee:** Producer name, region, country, elevation (on some), process, roast level (numeric scale), tasting notes, available grind options.

**What they do NOT publish:** Cupping scores, FOB prices, green cost, or detailed cost breakdowns.

## 2. Saint Frank Single-Origin Espresso Program

### Scale & Breadth

Saint Frank currently offers approximately **8 single-origin coffees** including:
- Bolivia Golondrina Anaerobic Gesha ($15/4oz)
- Guatemala Guayo's Gesha ($27/10oz)
- Honduras Finca Los Amigos ($21/10oz)
- Burundi Ninga Hill Natural Anaerobic ($26/10oz)
- **Kenya Thuti Espresso** ($23/10oz) -- **dedicated SO espresso**
- Honduras Las Nubes ($24/10oz)
- Burundi Ubuntu Decaf ($22/10oz)
- Kenya Karnewthi ($22/10oz)

This is a **smaller but curated** selection compared to Verve's ~12 offerings.

### Dedicated Single-Origin Espresso

Saint Frank rotates 1-2 single-origin espressos at a time. Recent and current offerings include:
- Kenya Thuti Espresso ($23/10oz) -- currently available
- Kenya Karuthi Espresso ($22/10oz) -- previously available
- Burundi Bumba Hill Natural -- reviewed positively as espresso
- Honduras Finca Los Amigos -- a Home-Barista user reported making it all as espresso successfully

Their approach of roasting each blend component separately for optimal solubility extends to how they develop single-origin espressos -- they aim for the most even extraction possible.

### Community Reception (Single-Origins)

Saint Frank's single-origin espresso reputation:
- Brian's Coffee Spot reviewed a Burundi Giku Hill Natural SOE as complex, well-balanced, with just a touch of bitterness
- Home-Barista user found Honduras beans "delish" as espresso
- The Infatuation and TripExpert reviewers focus more on the cafe experience than the beans-for-home-espresso angle
- **Less online discussion overall** -- the boutique size means fewer data points

### Roast Approach for Single-Origins

Saint Frank roasts to the **light-medium to medium** range. Their philosophy prioritizes origin character -- they describe wanting the coffee to express the terroir and the producer's work. Las Nieves Honduras is labeled light-medium. They use a single roastery at their Mission Street location in SF, which allows tighter quality control on smaller batches.

### Sourcing Transparency

Saint Frank's **Relational Sourcing** model is genuinely distinctive:
- **Transparency Report** (published annually) covers 100% of purchases
- Lists: producer name, region, harvest period, relationship start year, exporters, importers, lot volume, cupping scores (origin scores), and FOB pricing
- Publishes percentage above concurrent market price (claims 2-4x market price consistently)
- Deliberately targets "places that are largely undesired and avoided for risk, lack of recognition, and costliness"
- Founder travels annually to visit producers in remote mountain communities, often without electricity
- Buys entire harvests or sufficient cooperative volume for relationship depth

**What they publish per coffee:** Producer, region, relationship start date, exporters/importers, lot volume, cupping score (origin), FOB price, % above market price.

**What they do NOT publish:** Detailed production cost breakdowns, roast Agtron values, or brew recipes.

**Verdict on Saint Frank's transparency:** Their pricing transparency (publishing FOB prices and % above market) is **stronger than Verve's** on the financial side. However, they do not publish roasting data (Agtron values, roast curves) or detailed brew guides like Onyx does.

## 3. Onyx Coffee Lab Single-Origin Espresso Program

### Scale & Breadth

Onyx operates the **most prolific single-origin program** of the three:
- **70-100 single-origin offerings released per year**
- New single-origins arriving every week
- Currently sources from **20+ producing countries**
- Their Roaster's Choice subscription is their most popular, featuring the freshest single-origins with a new coffee every Monday/Tuesday

However, their **espresso-specific** collection is more limited at any given time. When checked, only 2 espresso blends (Geometry, Monarch) were in stock, with several single-origin espressos (El Salvador Santa Rosa Honey, Bolivia 4 Llamas Natural, Ecuador Hacienda La Papaya Sidra) sold out. This reflects their small-batch, high-demand model -- individual lots sell out quickly.

### Dedicated Single-Origin Espresso

Onyx approaches espresso differently than Verve or Saint Frank:
- Their **cafe espresso** is pulled with Geometry (Expressive Light, Agtron #135) -- an extremely light roast by industry standards
- **Delta Spirit** ($16/8oz, $36/27oz) is their dedicated medium-roast espresso blend at Agtron #65.5
- **Monarch** ($22.50/10oz) is their milk-drink espresso at Agtron #63 (Expressive Dark)
- Rotating single-origin espressos appear and sell out rapidly (often within days)

**Key distinction:** While Onyx's permanent espresso lineup is blend-focused (Geometry, Monarch, Delta Spirit), their rotating single-origins are where the community excitement lives. These are competition-grade coffees -- individual producer lots, often processed experimentally (anaerobic, honey, natural), and they sell out fast.

### Community Reception (Single-Origins)

Onyx has the **strongest community reputation** of the three for single-origin quality:
- Tropical Weather called "probably my favorite blend of all time for espresso" by community members
- Their staff consistently place top-5 in the U.S. Barista Championship (8 consecutive years)
- Co-founder Andrea Allen won the 2020 U.S. Barista Championship and was runner-up at the 2021 World Barista Championship
- Morgan Eckroth won the 2022 U.S. Barista Championship and later joined Onyx
- Reddit consensus: "probably one of the most reputable [roasters] and does roast and source some exceptional coffees"
- Some criticism: 10oz bags barely enough to dial in for espresso; pricing perceived as premium
- Some users find the ultra-light roasts too fruity for traditional espresso palates

### Roast Approach for Single-Origins

Onyx's roast philosophy is the **lightest of the three**, using a proprietary classification system:

| Onyx Classification | Agtron (Ground) | Industry Equivalent |
|---------------------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Expressive Light | 125-135 | Ultra-light / Nordic |
| Light | ~100-110 | Light |
| Moderate | ~65 (whole bean) | Medium |
| Expressive Dark | ~63 (whole bean) | Medium-dark |

They describe their "Expressive Light" coffees as having a style "also known as Nordic-style roasting." For single-origins, they adjust roasts by half a degree or seconds to balance bright acidity and sweetness. They use **Loring S70 Peregrine** roasters for light profiles and **Diedrich CR-70** drum roasters for darker profiles (like Monarch) -- different machines optimized for different roast levels.

**This matters for home espresso:** Most of Onyx's single-origins require a profiling machine (like a [Gaggiuino](../machine/Gaggiuino-Modification-Overview.md)) to extract properly. On a stock 9-bar machine, their Expressive Light coffees will taste sour and thin. Profile matching by bean character is covered in [Profile Selection by Bean Origin](../profiles/Gaggiuino-Profile-Selection-by-Bean-Origin-and-Character.md).

### Sourcing Transparency

Onyx's transparency is **the industry benchmark** among US specialty roasters:

**What they publish per coffee:**
- Producer name, farm, region, country
- Elevation (MASL)
- Coffee variety (e.g., SL-28, Pink Bourbon, Gesha)
- Process method with technical detail (e.g., "5 Day Anaerobic Natural, Raised Bed & Mechanical Dried")
- Harvest season
- Cupping score (SCA scale, 86+ minimum purchasing standard)
- **Green cost (price paid for green coffee)**
- **FOB pricing**
- **Transportation cost** (e.g., "$1.01 from New Jersey to Arkansas")
- **Production cost per pound** (labor, packaging, roast loss)
- Roaster model used (Diedrich CR-35, Loring S70, etc.)
- Agtron roast level (both whole bean and ground)
- Caffeine content per 12oz cup
- **Brew recipes** (dose, yield, ratio, time) for espresso and filter

Example from a Cup of Excellence lot (Costa Rica Volcan Azul):
- Auction price: $32/lb
- Green cost: $32.00
- Transportation: $1.01
- Production cost: ~$4.85/lb
- Retail: $35/4oz, $60/10oz

**This level of transparency is unmatched.** Neither Verve nor Saint Frank publishes production costs, roaster models, Agtron values, or brew recipes per coffee.

## 5. Correcting the Verve vs Saint Frank Framing

The earlier (unpublished) draft presented a "Who Chooses Which" table that positioned:
- Verve = reliable blends, easy ordering
- Saint Frank = interesting single-origin espresso

This framing is **incomplete**. Here is a more accurate picture:

### What Was Correct
- Saint Frank's single-origin espressos are praised for nuance and quality
- Verve's blends (Sermon, Streetlevel) are community favorites for espresso
- Saint Frank has smaller-batch advantage for quality control
- Verve has better logistics and wider availability

### What Was Overstated
- **Saint Frank does NOT have a clear single-origin advantage over Verve.** Verve currently offers more single-origin coffees (~12 vs ~8), has a dedicated single-origin espresso (Ethiopia Anasora), and runs a premium micro-lot program (Farmlevel Reserve) that directly competes at the boutique level.
- The perception of Saint Frank as "the single-origin choice" may be driven by their **smaller size** and **cafe identity** (they prominently feature rotating SO espresso at their cafes) rather than a genuinely superior online SO program.

### What Was Understated
- **Onyx belongs in this comparison.** For single-origin espresso quality, transparency, and competition pedigree, Onyx is arguably superior to both Verve and Saint Frank. The omission of Onyx from the original comparison left out the strongest competitor.
- **Verve's Farmlevel Reserve program** represents genuine micro-lot work at the $35-$38/150g tier, comparable to what boutique roasters offer. This was not mentioned in the original note.

### Updated "Who Chooses Which" Table

| If you want... | Choose |
|----------------|--------|
| Largest active SO selection online | **Verve** (~12 at any time, weekly rotation) |
| Most SO volume annually | **Onyx** (70-100/year, but they sell out fast) |
| Dedicated SO espresso offerings | **All three** offer this; Onyx and Saint Frank rotate more frequently |
| Most transparent sourcing | **Onyx** (cupping scores, green cost, brew recipes, Agtron) |
| Financial transparency (prices paid to farmers) | **Saint Frank** (FOB prices, % above market) then **Onyx** |
| Competition-validated quality | **Onyx** (8 years top-5 USBC, World Championship placements) |
| Best value SO espresso | **Verve** (12oz bags, 5lb bulk, free subscription shipping) |
| Boutique relationship-first sourcing | **Saint Frank** (smallest operation, founder visits producers annually) |
| Works on stock Gaggia (no profiling) | **Saint Frank** (medium roasts) > **Verve** (light but manageable) > **Onyx** (many require profiling) |
| Best with [Gaggiuino](../machine/Gaggiuino-Modification-Overview.md) profiling | **Onyx** (light roasts designed for profiling machines) |

## 6. Community Discussion Summary

### Head-to-Head Threads

Direct three-way comparisons of Verve, Saint Frank, and Onyx are **rare** in online forums. The roasters occupy different niches:
- **Onyx** is discussed nationally alongside Heart, George Howell, Counter Culture, and Intelligentsia -- the "elite tier" of US specialty roasters
- **Verve** is discussed regionally (Bay Area / West Coast) alongside Ritual, Four Barrel, Sightglass, and Blue Bottle -- the "West Coast establishment"
- **Saint Frank** is discussed locally (SF Bay Area) alongside Equator, Andytown, and Wrecking Ball -- the "respected boutique tier"

When roasters are ranked in community threads, Onyx tends to appear in a tier above both Verve and Saint Frank. One Medium article was titled "My Top 5 Coffee Roasters (That aren't Onyx)" -- implying Onyx is the assumed #1 that needs no mention.

### Reddit Consensus (Aggregated)

Based on GummySearch analysis of 171+ reviews from r/espresso and 27+ related subreddits:
- **Onyx:** "Probably one of the most reputable [roasters] and does roast and source some exceptional coffees"
- **Verve:** Named Roaster of the Year 2024; praised for balance between quality and accessibility; listed among "best single-origin coffee subscriptions"
- **Saint Frank:** Rarely mentioned on Reddit -- too small for the national conversation. Praised locally in SF-specific threads.

## 7. Recommendation for Home Espresso Setup

For the [Gaggia Classic Evo Pro](../machine/Gaggia-Classic-Evo-Pro-Best-Practices.md) + DF64 Gen2 grinder + [Gaggiuino](../machine/Gaggiuino-Modification-Overview.md) setup:

### Primary Rotation (Single-Origin Espresso)

1. **Onyx rotating single-origins** -- Set up Roaster's Choice subscription ($22.50/10oz) for weekly new single-origins. Match profiles via [Profile Selection by Bean Origin](../profiles/Gaggiuino-Profile-Selection-by-Bean-Origin-and-Character.md). Best exploits the Gaggiuino's profiling capability.

2. **Verve Ethiopia Anasora Natural Espresso** ($27.50/12oz) or whatever their current SO espresso is -- Light roast, origin-forward, good value in 12oz bags. Works well with blooming or IUIUIU profiles on Gaggiuino.

3. **Saint Frank Kenya Thuti Espresso** ($23/10oz) or their current SO espresso rotation -- Medium-roast, forgiving, works even without profiling. Good contrast to the lighter Onyx/Verve offerings.
